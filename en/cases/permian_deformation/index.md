# Monitoring Surface Deformation Associated with Oil and Gas Production in the Permian Basin with Time-Series InSAR

The cumulative deformation results reveal a pronounced subsidence signal in the Pecos area and its surroundings. Between November 2014 and January 2017, cumulative line-of-sight (LOS) subsidence in excess of 5 cm had already developed in this region. By January 2019, the cumulative subsidence exceeded 10 cm in localized areas, while the spatial extent of the subsidence signal expanded by approximately a factor of four. These observations are generally consistent with the results reported by Staniewicz et al. (2020), who identified approximately 13 cm of cumulative subsidence between 2014 and 2019 in an area located about 15 km south of Pecos, accompanied by a continuously increasing deformation trend. The location of the subsidence center and the magnitude of deformation obtained in this study agree well with previous observations, indicating the persistence of substantial land subsidence in the Pecos region over the study period.

To reduce the influence of residual atmospheric delays on the InSAR deformation estimates, a median absolute deviation (MAD)-based outlier detection and removal approach was applied. This method identifies interferograms strongly affected by anomalous atmospheric conditions, such as convective weather events and heat waves, and excludes them from the subsequent stacking process. Because atmospheric artifacts commonly manifest as long-wavelength spatial errors, contaminated interferograms can introduce large-scale spurious signals into cumulative deformation maps. After outlier removal, long-wavelength banding patterns and background fluctuations were substantially reduced, and the subsidence centers and their spatial characteristics became more clearly defined. These results demonstrate that the proposed approach effectively mitigates residual atmospheric noise and improves the reliability of the deformation measurements.

The ascending and descending track results show good agreement over the principal subsidence area south of Pecos. Both viewing geometries reveal a distinct subsidence center with comparable deformation magnitudes, suggesting that vertical motion dominates in this region. In contrast, noticeable differences are observed between the ascending and descending measurements near Pecos and to its north. Because ascending and descending geometries have different sensitivities to east-west motion, horizontal displacement produces different LOS responses in the two datasets. The observed discrepancies therefore likely indicate the coexistence of vertical subsidence and horizontal ground motion. This suggests that the deformation cannot be explained solely by one-dimensional reservoir compaction and may instead reflect the combined effects of fault activity and reservoir compaction. In particular, pore-pressure reduction associated with hydrocarbon production can lead to reservoir compaction, while stress redistribution may promote normal fault slip, generating additional vertical and horizontal deformation.

The progressively increasing subsidence observed in the Pecos area is likely associated with the rapid expansion of oil and gas development in the Permian Basin over the past decade. Deformation rates increased markedly between 2017 and 2019, coinciding with a period of rapid growth in unconventional hydrocarbon production. The acceleration of subsidence observed after 2017 may therefore reflect the combined influence of intensified resource extraction and the associated geomechanical response of the subsurface.
